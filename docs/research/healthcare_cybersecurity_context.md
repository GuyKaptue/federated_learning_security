# Healthcare cybersecurity context for federated learning (FL)

**Scope and honesty.** This document frames why the sprint's experiments matter for healthcare. It is a research framing, **not legal advice and not a compliance statement**. Legal sources are cited as pointers. Legal texts were **partly read on 2026-11-06** (details per act below and in REFERENCES_MASTER.md); anything not listed as read was not read, and a consolidated or amended version may differ. Nothing in this sprint was run on patient data, with hospitals, or inside a clinical network: all experiments use synthetic data, small public research tables, and four public heart-disease cohorts, with **virtual clients simulated in one process**.

## 1. Why FL in healthcare
Health data is special-category personal data (GDPR Art. 9). FL lets sites train a shared model without pooling raw records, but FL alone is not a privacy or security guarantee: updates leak (gradient leakage, membership inference), participants can poison the model (backdoors, Byzantine updates), and the server is a trust anchor.

## 2. Security objectives mapped to sprint evidence
| Objective | Threat | Sprint mechanism studied | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confidentiality of updates vs. server | honest-but-curious aggregator | HE (CKKS/BFV/Paillier), SecAgg, SMPC | FL-SEC-001 |
| Confidentiality vs. output inference | membership inference, gradient inversion | client-side DP / clipping+noise (measured utility/privacy trade-off) | FL-ATTACK-003 |
| Integrity of the global model | backdoor, Byzantine clients | robust aggregation, clipping, anomaly filtering, SMPC norm bound | FL-ATTACK-003 |
| Availability | client dropout, cost at scale | SecAgg dropout recovery, scalability benchmarks | FL-SEC-001/-PERF-002 |
| Auditability / reproducibility | undetected result drift | provenance hashes, deterministic seeds | FL-PUB-005 |

## 3. Regulatory pointers (not verified, no compliance claim)
- **GDPR** (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) — *read from the legislation.gov.uk copy of the as-adopted text (not the EUR-Lex OJ page, which rendered only recitals)*: **Art. 9(1)** prohibits processing of special categories (health data among them) unless a paragraph-2 exception applies; read exceptions: **9(2)(a)** explicit consent, **(h)** preventive/occupational medicine and health or social care under law or a contract with a health professional, **(i)** public-interest public health, **(j)** archiving, scientific or historical research and statistical purposes under law with suitable safeguards; **9(3)** professional-secrecy condition for (h); **9(4)** Member States may maintain further conditions for health data. **Art. 32(1)** lists as possible measures (a) pseudonymisation and encryption, (b) ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience, (c) timely restoration of availability, (d) a process for regularly testing and evaluating the measures; key phrases of Art. 25(1) and 35(1) were also seen. *Not read:* the other articles, the current consolidated EU text, member-state research rules. Whether FL training falls under 9(2)(j) or another ground is a legal question not analysed.
- **EU AI Act** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) — **Art. 15(1)-(5)** read from artificialintelligenceact.eu (an *unofficial* rendering): appropriate accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity over the lifecycle; accuracy metrics declared in instructions of use; resilience to errors and feedback loops; resilience against unauthorised third parties exploiting vulnerabilities, with measures against data poisoning, model poisoning, adversarial examples/model evasion and confidentiality attacks. *Not read:* Art. 6, Annexes (high-risk classification of medical-device AI), later amendments (e.g. the digital omnibus proposal).
- **NIS2** (Directive (EU) 2022/2555) — read from the EUR-Lex consolidated text: **Art. 21(1)** appropriate and proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures; **Art. 21(2)** all-hazards approach with (a) risk analysis and system-security policies, (b) incident handling, (c) business continuity, (d) supply-chain security, (e) secure acquisition/development/maintenance, (f) effectiveness assessment, (g) cyber hygiene and training, (h) cryptography/encryption policies, (i) HR security, access control, asset management, (j) multi-factor/continuous authentication; **Annex I sector 5 (health)** lists healthcare providers, EU reference laboratories, pharmaceutical R&D, pharmaceutical manufacturing and critical medical-device manufacturers. *Not read:* the rest of the directive and any national transposition; whether a given hospital is an essential or important entity depends on size and national law.
- **Germany, BDSG §22 (read from gesetze-im-internet.de, 2026-11-06):** §22(1) permits, notwithstanding Art. 9(1) GDPR, processing of special categories by public and non-public bodies where, among other grounds, it is necessary for health care, medical diagnosis, care or treatment, or management of health and social care systems and the data are processed by or under the responsibility of persons under a professional secrecy obligation (Nr. 1 lit. b), or for public-interest reasons in public health incl. quality/safety standards of health care, medicinal products and medical devices (Nr. 1 lit. c). §22(2) requires appropriate and specific measures to protect the data subject's interests (list read only by heading/summary, not verbatim here). **This is care/public-health oriented; whether any of it covers FL model *research/training* use is a legal question not analysed here (research-specific provisions were not read).** Other German sector rules (Länder hospital laws, SGB V, KRITIS/BSI) were not researched.
Using FL, HE, SecAgg or DP does **not** by itself establish compliance with any of the above; that requires a legal/DPIA assessment by qualified people.

## 4. Gap between this simulation and a hospital deployment
Virtual clients share one process and one RNG discipline; there is no network, no mutual TLS, no key management/HSM, no identity/attestation of sites, no real label/feature heterogeneity, no clinical data, and no side-channel analysis. Communication numbers are *logical payload bytes*, not network traffic. Results transfer to hospitals only as hypotheses to be re-tested.
