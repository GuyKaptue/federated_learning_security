# Research gap (summary; details in `scientific_gap_analysis.md`)

The manuscript shows that, against omniscient colluding attackers on one synthetic benchmark, fixed robust rules are often beaten by a rule-simulating attacker. It leaves open: (1) whether a history-aware or learned attacker adds anything over our fixed-candidate search; (2) whether diversified colluders evade similarity defences; (3) how natural healthcare heterogeneity changes detection. The literature already contains a multi-round attacker (PoisonedFL), RL-based attackers and defences (Li et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2025), a 15-attack/17-defence benchmark (FLPoison) and a survey of heterogeneity effects. A new contribution therefore has to be an *evidence-based comparison in the cross-silo, small-K, healthcare-motivated setting*, not a new attack principle. Negative results are acceptable and expected to be reported.
